Research

My research spans seven interconnected lines, from two major active projects with several papers in flight to a signature line published continuously since 2010, alongside established themes in China’s land policy, real estate finance, and environmental sustainability, and a newer methodological direction. What connects them is a way of working: turning large spatial datasets into questions about place, behaviour, and inequality. Where a project lends itself to it, I publish an interactive map so the patterns can be explored directly rather than only read about. A growing collection is gathered below.

Research Areas

My research spans seven interconnected areas. Each page below gathers its introduction, collaborators, events, publications, working papers, and interactive maps.

AI and computational methods for spatial analysis New data and inference techniques treated as research objects in their own right: their capabilities, failure modes and responsible deployment.
Exclusion and spatial inequality in housing markets How housing markets exclude, by prejudice, gatekeeping or scarcity, and how spatial constraints turn individual decisions into population-level inequality.
Spatial personality Estimating Big Five personality at scale from digital traces, and mapping how regional personality shapes entrepreneurship, mobility and resilience.
Spatial analysis of environment and climate Spatial measurement where official statistics are thin, and whether environmental commitments survive contact with incentives.
Spatial analysis of real estate and finance Extracting reliable price signals from thin, irregularly traded stock, and asking what actually moves property values.
Behavioural economics of housing decisions Why households depart from textbook-rational behaviour in housing decisions, and what reference dependence and loss aversion mean for policy.
Land use policy in China China's distinctive rural and urban land regimes, and their consequences for inequality, environmental quality and productivity.

Working with me

I supervise doctoral candidates on the above themes. Prospective students should consult the Cambridge Land Economy PhD programme page for admissions information and contact me directly to discuss fit before applying.

I welcome collaboration enquiries from researchers in adjacent fields such as applied psychology, computer science, urban planning, behavioural public administration. The Lab for Interdisciplinary Spatial Analysis is the institutional home for such work.

For specific paper inquiries or media requests, email me.
