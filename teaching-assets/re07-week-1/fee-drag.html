A worked example of fee drag

An investor can hold a regulated infrastructure asset directly, or through a closed-end fund that sells it after ten years and starts again in a new fund. This page prices that choice. The inputs are real figures: the returns that regulators allow on equity, the fees that fund managers charge, the fund life, and the net returns that infrastructure funds have actually delivered. Move the sliders and see where the gross return goes.

RE07 Global Infrastructure Investment and Management, Week 1. Every default and every preset is taken from a published source listed at the foot of the page, with the page number. The model is deliberately simple (one commitment drawn at the start, fees taken from the fund’s assets, a whole-of-fund waterfall, no leverage or tax) and it is a teaching illustration and not a forecast. It was computed twice, in Python and in this page, and the two agree.

What the observed returns of infrastructure funds imply

Andonov, Kräussl and Rauh (2021) report the net internal rate of return of 210 infrastructure funds with performance data: mean 9.92 per cent, median 9.35 per cent, against a public market equivalent of 0.93 (Table 3, panel B, page 3897). The page finds the gross return that a fund on the terms you have set would need to earn to deliver that net figure over one fund life. It is a calculation under a stated model and not a measurement, because real funds draw capital over several years, use leverage and are not all on the same terms.

  • The model takes the management fee out of the fund’s assets and applies the hurdle to the value after fees. Real partnerships call fees on top of the commitment and measure the hurdle from each drawdown, so the timing differs, but the size of the drag is similar.
  • With catch-up, the net return rises very little over a range of gross returns just above the hurdle, because the manager takes the extra. The flat stretch on the third chart is the catch-up. Switch it off to see the difference.
  • The direct route is not free. An internal team has to be built, and the default cost is an average for all asset classes. Raise it and see how much of the advantage survives.
  • The fund route can be worth its cost if it gives access to assets that the investor could not otherwise reach or manage. The page measures the price, not the value.
