What the charts measure

Each chart shows how far a sector’s share of a city’s gross value added (GVA) has moved, in percentage points, not how fast the sector has grown. A sector can grow in money terms and still lose share if the rest of the economy grows faster.

Definitions

  • Real estate (all five cities): real estate activities including the imputed rent of owner-occupiers, because the international data do not separate it. The imputed part moves with house prices. For New York it is “real estate and rental and leasing”; the BEA suppresses a few small-county cells, so New York’s share is slightly understated.
  • Knowledge-intensive services (all five cities): information and communication, finance and insurance, and professional and business services (including administrative services). For New York the BEA’s professional and business services stands in for the last group.
  • Finance and insurance (all five cities): financial and insurance activities (NACE section K; for New York the BEA's finance and insurance). It is part of knowledge-intensive services, so the two lines overlap.
  • London, ONS definitions (the line chart on slide 10): real estate excluding imputed rent, and knowledge-intensive exporting services as information and communication, finance and insurance, and professional, scientific and technical activities. These can be shown for London only.
  • Paris is the Ile-de-France region, the geography the Centre for Cities report states. Brussels data begin in 2003.

Why it matters

In the framework of Murphy, Shleifer and Vishny, talent that moves into activities that redistribute wealth rather than create it slows growth. The Centre for Cities asks whether London’s economy is tilting that way. A rising real estate share alongside a stalling knowledge-services share would be the warning sign; the page lets you see how sensitive that reading is to the period chosen.
