Do macroprudential tools work on housing markets?

Choose a policy tool and a group of economies to see how often it was used, then read how it is supposed to work and what the evidence says, and whether that evidence changes with the period.

RE02 Real Estate Finance and Investment, Michaelmas Week 2. Policy actions for 60 economies: 2000 to 2012 from the BIS database in Shim, I., Bogdanova, B., Shek, J. and Subelyte, A. (2013), “Database for policy actions on housing markets”, BIS Quarterly Review, September; 2013 to 2024 from the IMF iMaPP database in Alam, Z. et al. (2019), “Digging deeper: evidence on the effects of macroprudential policies from a new database”, IMF Working Paper 19/66. Evidence: Kuttner, K. and Shim, I. (2016), “Can non-interest rate policies stabilise housing markets? Evidence from a panel of 57 economies”, Journal of Financial Stability 26, 31–44, and an update using the same event-study design.

How many economies? The policy actions cover the 60 economies of the BIS database, and the chart averages over the economies covered in each year (55 to 60). Kuttner and Shim’s estimates use 57 economies from that set, those with house price and credit data. Our update uses the 54 economies with house price data and the 43 with household credit data.
