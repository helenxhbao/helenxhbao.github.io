The Geography of Housing Demand in Britain

Revealed housing demand from ~294 million property-search enquiries (2010–2025), summarised as a family of search-flow indices for each travel-to-work area (TTWA). Choose a year, or all years, together with a market and an index to colour the map; choose a travel-to-work area to see where its residents search and who searches into it, or all areas for the national picture. The tables beside the map follow the chosen index: search flows for the flow measures, and the highest and lowest areas for the rest. Search signals interest, not a completed move; enquirer-location coverage is high from 2018 and thinner before, and England is the reliable canvas.

The nine indices each capture a different facet of an area's search. Outflow is the number of cross-boundary enquiries its residents make about property elsewhere. Inflow is the number of enquiries coming into the area from people who live elsewhere. Retained is the number of enquiries in which the searcher and the property both sit inside the area. The leaving rate is the share of residents' own search that points outside the area. Net-flow, running from −1 to +1, records whether more search arrives than departs, so a positive value marks a net sink and a negative value a net source. Openness is the share of all the area's search that crosses its boundary in either direction. Reach is the mean distance, in kilometres, that residents look when they search beyond home. Concentration, a Herfindahl index from 0 to 1, records whether residents' outward search is spread widely across destinations or funnelled into a few. Search volume is simply the total number of enquiries originating in the area.

Colour shows the selected index for the chosen year and market; the outlined area is the selected TTWA. Hover a TTWA for its value, or click it to load its search flows in the tables alongside. Grey = no reliable data (mainly Scotland and Wales).
